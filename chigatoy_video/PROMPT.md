# Video kit: "Chig'atoy ulusining bo'linishi" (9:16, about 40 s)

`map_blackboard.png` is the blackboard map. Use it as the reference or start image so the map
and its labels come out right; video models usually scramble text they draw themselves.
Rebuild it with `python3 chigatoy_video/make_map.py`.

## Why the script is split into clips

Read aloud, the full script takes about 35–45 seconds. Most text-to-video models make
clips of 5–10 seconds. Veo 3, for example, stops at 8 seconds. Generate the 6 clips
below separately, using the same character block every time, then join them in CapCut
or a similar editor.

Numbers are written out as words because models mispronounce "XIV", "1348" and "8-E".

## Character and scene block (paste at the start of EVERY clip)

> A cheerful 13-year-old Uzbek schoolboy with short dark hair, in a neat school uniform:
> white shirt, dark blue jacket, dark trousers. He stands in a bright classroom in front
> of a green blackboard that shows a hand-drawn chalk map of Central Asia: a yellow region
> labeled MOVAROUNNAHR on the left (west) and a blue region labeled MO'G'ULISTON on the
> right (east), separated by a dashed pink line. Warm daylight from the windows, medium
> shot, vertical 9:16, realistic style, natural hand gestures, clear lip sync, speaking
> Uzbek. No subtitles, no on-screen text other than the blackboard.

## Getting the Uzbek speech right

Few video models handle Uzbek pronunciation and lip sync well. If the speech sounds wrong:

1. Record the script in your own voice on a phone (quiet room, about 40 s).
2. Make the video without speech (ask for "silent, mouth closed, natural gestures"), or use a
   single still image of the boy at the board.
3. Use an audio-driven lip-sync or talking-avatar tool. You upload the image or video plus your
   recording, and it moves the mouth to match.

That also gives you your real voice, which suits a school presentation.

## History check

The script matches the standard school account:
- Kebek and Tarmashirin, khans of the Chagatai Ulus, moved its centre to Movarounnahr.
  Tarmashirin adopted Islam, and the eastern nomadic nobles revolted against him in 1334
  to defend the old Mongol order (the Yasa).
- Around 1346–1347 Amir Qazag'an took control of Movarounnahr, and the ulus split in two.
- In 1347/1348 the Dughlat emirs enthroned Tug'luq Temur as khan of Mo'g'uliston
  (Yettisuv and Eastern Turkestan). Uzbek textbooks give 1348; some sources say 1347.
- Optional detail: Tug'luq Temur later converted to Islam himself (about 1354).
