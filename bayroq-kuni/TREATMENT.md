# Bayroq kuni — treatment

**Style:** Silkscreen Travel Poster (`silkscreen-poster`), used as an *event poster*.
**Topic:** 18 November, the day the State Flag of the Republic of Uzbekistan was adopted (1991).
**Format:** 1080×1920 (vertical, 9:16), 24 fps, ~60 s. Uzbek (Latin script). No narration: the printed text is the subtitle (STYLE §4). Original score, no anthem melody.

## 1. Three structures, and the choice

1. **One ink, one meaning.** A single portrait poster is printed on camera, one ink per pull. Every ink is a colour of the flag, and each pull is followed by a small proof card on the bench that names the colour and what it stands for. The crescent and twelve stars are not printed at all: they are holes in the blue screen, bare paper. Registration slips before the last pull, snaps, and the date prints in the dark info band. The print wakes up at the end.
2. **One poster, many runs.** The same poster re-inked dawn → noon → night (three palettes), the flag the one thing that never changes colour. Strong "time" idea, but re-inking would put the flag in wrong colours or demand they stay fixed; the meanings of the colours, which are the content, have nowhere to go.
3. **A street walk.** A multi-plane truck down a festive street, each flag on a balcony printed as we pass. Lively, but the flags would be small and the meanings would turn into floating captions.

**Chosen: 1.** The meanings of the colours *are* the information, and silkscreen prints exactly one colour at a time: the medium gives us the order of facts for free. The flag's white, crescent and stars being unprinted paper is the style's "paper as a colour" carrying the meaning of purity and peace. Opening, ending and score shape come from the topic: it opens on a pale sky being pulled (the sky the blue stands for), ends on the flag waving in a printed wind among a print run of copies laid out on the bench (one for every home).

## 2. Logline and arc

A blank sheet becomes Uzbekistan's flag one ink at a time, each colour saying what it stands for, until the layers lock into register on the date it was adopted and the printed flag starts to wave.

- **Setup:** pale sky pulled; blue (sky, water), green (nature), white (unprinted paper), red (life force).
- **Turn:** the canton: the crescent and twelve stars were never ink, only paper left open. Then every layer slips loose — silence — and snaps into register.
- **Ending:** the dark ink prints the date. The camera pulls back: copies lie all around on the bench, and every printed flag waves in the same wind.

## 3. Benchmark

- **1930s–40s WPA national-park screen prints** — *learn:* 4–6 flat inks, stepped-band skies, the dark info band at the foot, value stepping from far to near. *Don't take:* any composition, lettering or park.
- **Mid-century animated title sequences (shapes arriving on the beat)** — *learn:* one graphic element per musical event, pauses before the title. *Don't take:* any shapes, type or music.

## 4. Palette (5 inks + paper)

| Role | Ink |
|---|---|
| paper (white stripe, crescent, stars, snow) | `#F4EEDF` cream stock |
| light sky | `#C9E5EC` (with a stepped band `#DCEEF1`) |
| flag blue (far mountains, river) | `#0099B5` |
| flag green (valley, fields) | `#1EB53A` |
| accent: flag red (the two thin lines only) | `#CE1126` |
| near / info band / pole / skyline | `#15304A` |

Flag geometry from the 500×250 construction: blue 80, red 5, white 80, red 5, green 80; crescent = circle r30 at (70,40) minus circle r30 at (80,40); 12 five-point stars r6 in rows of 3 / 4 / 5, right-aligned (x 136–184, 112–184, 88–184; y 16, 40, 64).

## 5. Shot list (camera moves: truck, locked, push, tilt-truck, pan, crane, push+tilt, log pull-back)

| # | t (s) | Framing / angle | Move | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.0–4.8 | close, 3° roll, frame edge in view | truck down with the squeegee as the sky split-fountain pulls; screen lifts, fast pull-back to the full sheet | hook: colour arriving in the first second |
| 2 | 4.8–12.0 | wide, top-down: sheet + card | locked; screen down, blue pull; card 1 slides in | watch the first flag colour land and read it |
| 3 | 12.0–19.2 | wide | locked, a slow creep; green pull; card 2 | the valley and the green stripe arrive together |
| 4 | 19.2–24.0 | medium on the white stripe | push into bare paper, now framed by blue and green | white is the one colour that is never printed |
| 5 | 24.0–28.8 | close, tilted 4°, along the flag | tilt-truck with the narrow red pull | the thinnest ink needs the closest look |
| 6 | 28.8–36.0 | close on the canton | push to the crescent | the crescent is a hole in the blue screen |
| 7 | 36.0–43.2 | close on the stars | lateral pan along the three rows while the card counts 1→12 | counting needs a path across them |
| 8 | 43.2–46.8 | medium, multi-plane | crane down through the loose layers (paper gaps, shadows) → snap | the turn: everything locks into register |
| 9 | 46.8–54.0 | the whole poster, then a gentle push + tilt toward the info band | the card stack slides off the bench; navy pull prints skyline, pole, header and band; date stamps left→right | reading the date on the finished print |
| 10 | 54.0–60.0 | log-zoom pull-back over the bench | eight more copies of the print appear around it; every printed flag starts to wave; the last card lands; final lockup ≥ 3.6 s | the poster in the world: a print run, one for every home |

Transitions are made of the medium: screen drops and lifts, the squeegee front, the snap, cards sliding over cards. No dissolves, no bare cuts, no fades.
The proof cards live in screen space at the foot of the vertical frame (like a ticket stub), so the camera can go close on the flag while the words stay put.

- 24.0 clack (narrow screen). 24.3–25.2 red pull, both lines. 25.5 lift. 25.8 card 4. 26.1 "QIZIL" / "hayotiy kuch irmoqlari". Hold to 28.8.

- 36.0 card 6 with a counter; 36.6–38.25 twelve ticks (one per star as the pan passes it), 38.4 "12 YULDUZ" / "yilning oʻn ikki oyi". Hold to 43.2.

## 7. Cue map

- **Tempo** 100 bpm, 4/4, D Dorian-flavoured, 25 bars.
- **Instruments** (Uzbek folk palette, played by hand): doira (frame drum: dum, tak, jingles) synthesized; dutor-like plucked two-string lute (Karplus–Strong); a nay-like flute line (sampled flute); a low drone (dutor open strings).
- **Sections:** A (bars 1–5) doira + dutor ostinato; B (bars 6–8) full: flute melody enters with green; C (bars 9–10) drone only for the white; bars 11–12 full again for red; D (bars 13–18) canton: lighter, flute alone over dutor, star ticks on the beat grid; E (bars 19–20) everything drops out, then the snap; F (bars 20–25) full ensemble, doira rolls under the date, ending on a long held chord with jingles.
- **Hits:** every screen clack on a downbeat; every pull start on a beat (a strum); card slides on beats; star ticks on sixteenths… slowed to eighths (12 × 0.15 s); the snap on a downbeat.

| Section | Ambience | Foley | Music |
|---|---|---|---|
| open | room tone | screen clack, squeegee on mesh, ink squelch, hinge | doira + dutor |
| blue | room tone | clack, squeegee, hinge, card slide, print stamp | doira + dutor |
| white | room tone only | card slide (soft), paper hiss | drone only |
| green | room tone | clack, squeegee, card slide, stamps | full + flute |
| red | room tone | short squeegee, clack, card | full |
| moon / stars | room tone | card, 12 paper ticks | flute + dutor |
| snap | room tone, paper creak | layer drift whoosh, clamp snap | silence → hit |
| date | room tone | clack, squeegee, three stamps | full, doira roll |
| end | wind, flag flutter (J-cut 0.6 s early) | paper whoosh of the pull-back, card slide | full → held D major chord |

## 9. Type

- Display: **Big Shoulders Display** (condensed, OFL) for the colour words, date and final line, tracked out.
- Labels: **Outfit 600** (geometric sans, OFL) for the meanings.
- Uzbek apostrophes: ʻ (U+02BB) in oʻ / gʻ if the fonts carry it, otherwise ‘.
- Text exists only as ink: on the proof cards (navy ink, swatch in the colour), in the info band (paper knocked out of the navy), on the end tag. Every text is printed by a small pull or a stamp, never faded.
